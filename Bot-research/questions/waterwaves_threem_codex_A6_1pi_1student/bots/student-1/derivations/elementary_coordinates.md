# Common-cubic coordinates on the six-point shell

Define transformed triples

$$
u_i=-\omega_i\quad (i=1,2,3),
\qquad
v_j=\omega_{3+j}\quad (j=1,2,3).
$$

Frequency and momentum conservation give

$$
\sum_i u_i=\sum_j v_j=:s,
\qquad
\sum_i u_i^2=\sum_j v_j^2.
$$

Consequently their second elementary symmetric polynomials also agree:

$$
\sum_{i<j}u_i u_j
=\frac{s^2-\sum_i u_i^2}{2}
=\frac{s^2-\sum_j v_j^2}{2}
=\sum_{i<j}v_i v_j=:p.
$$

Writing

$$
r=\prod_i u_i,\qquad t=\prod_j v_j,
$$

the two root polynomials are therefore

$$
F_u(z)=\prod_i(z-u_i)=z^3-sz^2+pz-r,
$$

$$
F_v(z)=\prod_j(z-v_j)=z^3-sz^2+pz-t.
$$

In particular,

$$
F_u(z)-F_v(z)=t-r
$$

is a constant.  The full four-dimensional on-shell manifold, after quotienting
within-set permutations, thus has the natural coordinates $(s,p,r,t)$.

There is also an exact resultant identity:

$$
\operatorname{Res}(F_u,F_v)
=\prod_{i,j}(u_i-v_j)
=(r-t)^3.
$$

Since $u_i-v_j=-(\omega_i+\omega_{3+j})$ and there are nine factors,

$$
\boxed{\;
\prod_{i=1}^{3}\prod_{j=4}^{6}(\omega_i+\omega_j)
=-(r-t)^3.
\;}
$$

Thus any apparent common product of mixed-pair frequency denominators
compresses to one cubic power.  Moreover, $r=t$ makes $F_u=F_v$, so the two
root multisets coincide and the point lies on a multiply degenerate
pairing/wall locus rather than a generic factorization surface.

Every ordinary polynomial that is symmetric separately in the minus and plus
legs reduces on shell to a polynomial in these four quantities.  With weights

$$
\deg s=1,\qquad \deg p=2,\qquad \deg r=\deg t=3,
$$

there are only $17$ monomials of weighted degree eight:

$$
s^a p^b r^c t^d,
\qquad
a+2b+3c+3d=8.
$$

The amplitude is invariant under reversing all frequencies.  Combining that
operation with the minus/plus set swap acts as $r\leftrightarrow t$ at fixed
$(s,p)$.  The invariant weighted-degree-eight polynomial space consequently
has dimension $12$: five monomials with $c+d=0$, three proportional to
$r+t$, and four generated by $r^2+t^2$ and $rt$.

This does not yet prove that a chamber expression for $A_6/i$ is polynomial;
factorization denominators or positive-part walls may remain.  It does show
that any polynomial numerator/piece should be compressed in these generators
before a large raw monomial representation is accepted.

## Exact invariant-basis diagnostics

On $600$ fresh exact BG kinematics, the common-$s,p$ equalities and the
resultant identity pass exactly at every point.  Direct exact rank tests give

$$
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\text{global dictionary}&\operatorname{rank}F&
\operatorname{rank}[F\mid A_6/i]\\ \hline
17\text{ unrestricted weighted-degree-eight monomials}&17&18\\
12\text{ swap-symmetric weighted-degree-eight monomials}&12&13
\end{array}
$$

so the amplitude is not a single global polynomial in $(s,p,r,t)$.

The natural resultant-denominator tests also fail exactly.  For

$$
\frac{A_6}{i}=\frac{N_d(s,p,r,t)}{(r-t)^d},
\qquad d=1,2,3,
$$

with the complete weighted-degree $8+3d$ numerator of the required
$r\leftrightarrow t$ parity, the exact feature/augmented ranks are

$$
12/13,\qquad36/37,\qquad36/37,
$$

respectively.  Therefore the resultant collapse is a useful coordinate
identity, but a global denominator $(r-t)^d$ alone does not encode the wall
dependence.

Within individual squared-merge words, the $17$-monomial polynomial test is
inconsistent for the highly sampled words
$\mathrm{MMPPPM}$, $\mathrm{PMMPPM}$, and $\mathrm{PPMMMP}$.
This is consistent with the separate $S_{ij}$ subdivisions inside a raw word.
